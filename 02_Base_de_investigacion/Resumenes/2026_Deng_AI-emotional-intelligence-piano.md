## Resumen (del articulo)

With the increasing use of artificial intelligence (AI) in higher music education, there is a growing need to evaluate whether intelligent feedback systems can meaningfully enhance expressive piano performance. This study investigates the research question: Does the use of an AI-based analytical performance assessment system lead to greater improvements in piano expressivity than traditional instruction? To address this question, we developed an intelligent performance-assessment system that applies modern BiLSTM attention technologies to analyze expressive qualities of piano playing, including dynamics, phrasing, and emotional conveyance. A total of 290 Chinese undergraduate piano students (aged 18–22) were randomly assigned to an AI-supported group (n = 145) or a traditional-instruction control group (n = 145) for a three-month training period. Expressivity was measured using a hybrid 100-point scoring system that integrated objective performance analytics with expert double-blind evaluations. Statistical analysis included paired and independent t-tests and effect-size estimation. The inter­ vention group demonstrated a substantial improvement in expressive performance (ΔM = 12.97; t(144) = − 21.64; p < .001; d = 1.80), indicating a large effect. By contrast, changes in the control group were minimal and statistically nonsignificant (p = .092; d = 0.14).
