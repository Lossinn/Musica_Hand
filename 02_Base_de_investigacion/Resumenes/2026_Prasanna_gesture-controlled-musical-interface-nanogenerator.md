## Resumen (del articulo)

Gesture-controlled musical interfaces (GCMIs) offer an innovative approach to interacting with sound through body movements, enabled by mechano-electric transducers composed of an intrinsic hybrid nanogenerator (IHNG). The IHNG was constructed through 0.3Ba0.7Ca0.3TiO3–0.7BaSn0.12Ti0.88O3 (BCST)/poly­ dimethylsiloxane (PDMS) composite film. Enhanced piezoelectric (d33 = 205 pC/N) and ferroelectric (Pr = 8.06 μC/cm2, Ec = 3.4 kV/cm) properties of BCST ceramic were observed by optimizing the mechanical load (10–50 kN), resulting in the generation of the nanopolar regions, rhombohedral–orthorhombic–tetragonal phase coex­ istence, and switchable domains. The IHNG device generates an output of 250 V and 2.4 mA/m2 under me­ chanical load, driven by the synergistic effects of piezoelectric and triboelectric mechanisms. Furthermore, the GCMI system realised through the IHNG-based smart glove functioned as a mechano-electric transducer to demonstrate the real-time air drum. 1. Introduction Gesture-controlled musical interfaces (GCMI) have gained signifi­ cant interest due to easy operation through gesture movements, costeffectiveness, and wide utility in various fields, including easy access for persons with disabilities. The GCMI allows the user to interact with the hardware unit through gestures, enabling control and instruction of musical sounds using a programmed set of conditions to execute the necessary tasks, or to request help from a third party.
